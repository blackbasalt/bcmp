{% load passport %} {% comment %} A space card: what BCMP knows about the selected space. It arrives in the right-hand panel of the floor screen by a separate request, so this is a chunk of markup rather than a screen — the plan and the tree do not go anywhere when it opens. The card is read top to bottom as an answer to three questions in order: что это за помещение (the heading and the marks beside it), сколько его и какого оно рода (the numbers), где оно стоит (the tree). Each answer is a block of its own with a hairline above it and a caption set in small capitals: the rail is narrow, and a reader who has come for one of the three must find it without reading the other two. The hairlines run from edge to edge of the card and each block carries its own side padding: the card is then a stack of leaves and not a column of boxes floating inside a box. A rule that stops short of the card's edge draws a second frame within the frame the card already has, and with four blocks on a rail 20rem wide those frames were the loudest thing on it. This is why the card body itself has no padding (`floor.html`) — the padding is spent here, once per block. The "Документы" and "Системы" sections are deliberately absent here: the tables behind them are empty, and a section promising data that does not exist is worse than its own absence. {% endcomment %} {% comment %} The heading is the space's name, and the marks under it are what is said about the space in one word each. The код is set in a monospace face and not among the fields below: it is an identifier, read character by character and compared against a drawing, a table and a contract — and it is the one thing on the card that is copied rather than read. «Арендопригодное» and «Общего пользования» stand as marks and not as rows «да / нет»: a row with «нет» in it takes as much of a narrow rail as one with «да» and says nothing. The mark of the absent property is its absence. {% endcomment %}

The plate is a band across the card and not a tile with rounded corners: it is one of the four blocks and is told from its neighbours by its tint alone. An empty field is shown as a dash — the same «— нет данных» as in the passport (#5): blank space can be read as a zero, a dash cannot. The dash stands here too, only set small and quiet: a площадь that has not been recorded is not a large figure, and in the face this plate gives to numbers it would shout across the card louder than any of them. Nought is a value and is printed large like any other — hence `is None` and not a plain truth test. {% endcomment %}

It is drawn rather than listed because that is what it says: the помещение lies between what is above it and what is inside it, and «Выше» and «Ниже» as two labels made the reader assemble that picture themselves. The words «выше» and «ниже» stay in the captions of the neighbours' группы for the reason they were chosen: the `parent` relation means both nesting (cubicles in a lavatory) and the grouping of neighbouring spaces (a vestibule and the offices behind it), and the card must not claim which of the two is at hand. What the rail draws is order, not ownership. The tree neighbours are buttons, not links: they re-fill this same panel rather than leading away from the floor screen the panel exists for. They select a space by the same means as a tree node and a contour on the plan (`_space_select.html`). {% endcomment %}
